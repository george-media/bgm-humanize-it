# Genre and destination

Read only the relevant section. Adapt to the supplied samples and brief rather than imposing a signature style.

## Academic and technical

Preserve terminology, methods, reproducible detail, citations and cautious conclusions. Retain precision and real trade-offs. Formal punctuation, passive voice and longer sentences may be appropriate. Explain for the actual audience. Do not force contractions, casual substitutions, fragments or categorical claims.

## Narrative, blog and essay

Retain an effective opening, scene or context, authorial perspective and coherent development. Nonfiction uses supplied real memories and opinions, with honest abstraction where detail is absent. Do not invent a changed mind, mandate a thesis-first or scene-first opening, or force uncertainty intervals and unresolved endings.

## Creative and lyrical

Allow requested fictional invention, dialogue, imagery, rhythm, anaphora, symmetry and expressive punctuation. Keep the fictional frame clear when needed. An ending should serve the piece. Do not flatten art for detector scores, impose lopsidedness or demand citations for invented scenes. Do not present fictional real-person experience or evidence as nonfiction.

## Professional communications

Make the purpose and practical ask easy to find. Keep supplied responsibilities, numbers, dates and commitments, with their uncertainty. Adapt warmth and tact to the relationship. No mandatory sentence position, paragraph cap or invented deadline. Internal bluntness must not leak into client copy.

### Teamwork Desk

Damian's destination-specific convention: draft customer-facing replies in HTML unless instructed otherwise. Address the client by their supplied first name; do not invent one. Use calm, professional, customer-safe wording. Omit manual sign-offs because Desk adds signatures automatically. Drafting does not authorise sending. These rules do not turn every email into HTML or prohibit requested sign-offs elsewhere.

## Short async messages

Keep concise, scannable prose at the team's register. Suitable abbreviations, approximation markers, fragments or lowercase may be used when requested or supported by context. Do not add artificial slang, interruptions, disorder, unrelated fourth items or unsupported details to simulate authenticity.
