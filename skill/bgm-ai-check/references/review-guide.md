# Contextual review and editing hand-off

Read for substantial reviews, sample comparison or a difficult genre decision. This is an editorial guide, not an authorship classifier or a mandatory nine-category scorecard.

## Test a proposed finding

Identify the exact passage and the problem it causes for this reader or brief. Consider its useful function before recommending a change. A word can be vague in one passage and a precise technical term in another. Explain the context rather than labelling vocabulary as inherently human or AI.

Use descriptive priorities when helpful: a material accuracy or meaning issue, a comprehension or brief mismatch, or an optional style preference. These indicate editing impact, not likelihood of AI use. Certainty belongs to the particular observation: a quoted phrase is demonstrably present; whether it feels generic may be a tentative judgement. Do not attach one global confidence label that suggests measured detection accuracy. Do not penalise short text with an arbitrary ceiling on confidence in an obvious spelling or arithmetic error.

Merge findings with the same cause. A repeated vague sentence should not become independent vocabulary, specificity, transition and rhythm penalties. If several passages illustrate one issue, show representative examples rather than inflate the finding count. Do not invent a quota of findings, demand a fix for every sentence or imply absence of findings proves a particular origin.

## Relevant lenses

| Lens | Useful editorial question | Legitimate exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| Wording and repetition | Is a phrase needlessly elaborate, redundant or too vague to carry its intended claim? | Established terminology, emphasis, quotations and approved boilerplate. |
| Rhythm and structure | Does organisation hide the practical ask, lose a link or make a passage unnecessarily hard to follow? | Repeated structures for emphasis, formal consistency, useful bullets, summaries and purposeful symmetry. |
| Specificity and evidence | Is an important claim missing support, an actor, a condition or a necessary detail? | Honest abstraction, source-limited material and explicit fiction; no compulsory number or personal anecdote. |
| Uncertainty and argument | Does the wording overstate support or obscure a genuine trade-off? | Warranted hedges, conditional commitments and balanced conclusions. |
| Voice and audience | Does the register conflict with the brief or relevant sample features? | Warmth, humour, empathy, discipline-specific language and the writer's useful idiosyncrasies. |

Do not substitute unusual synonyms for precision. Useful transitions are not automatically padding. “Robust regression”, an evidence-based “faster than” comparison and a three-item safety list can all be appropriate. A passive construction can focus on the procedure when the actor is known or irrelevant. An em dash can clarify an aside; a semicolon can connect related clauses. House style governs authored typography without rewriting literal quotations.

## Genre and source boundaries

Academic, technical and legal prose may require formal terminology, cautious claims and consistent structure. A team message need not be disorderly, lowercase or slang-filled. Narrative can begin with a scene or a thesis; assess the effect, not the opening's category. Creative work permits requested invention, rhythm, anaphora and expressive punctuation. Nonfiction must not invent real memories, quotations, expertise or evidence.

Do not infer native-language background, a particular model or dishonest conduct from prose. If the register or language is unfamiliar, narrow the review to observations you can support or ask for the intended audience. Do not treat unfamiliar dialect, a translated sentence, accessibility wording or formulaic compliance language as evidence of authorship. Comparison to samples establishes textual similarities or differences, not identity or the amount of AI editing.

For Teamwork Desk copy, apply Damian's destination convention when relevant: client-facing HTML unless instructed otherwise, supplied first name, calm professional tone and no manual sign-off because Desk adds signatures. Review against that destination without importing internal bluntness or sending the draft. Other emails may use different formatting and requested signatures.

## Example report shape

Use only what helps the request. A short review can be a paragraph; a substantial one may use a table:

| Passage or location | Issue and effect | Suggested action |
|---|---|---|
| Exact short quote | Explain the demonstrated problem, or label a preference as optional | Minimal change, essential question, verification step or retain as written |

An overall assessment describes editing needs, not authorship. Avoid headings such as “What gave it away” that presume detection. Do not add a stock detector disclaimer to every ordinary review. Explain the attribution limit directly when it matters to the user's question.

## Safe editing hand-off

When editing is requested, load BGM Humanize It through its available skill entry. Hand it the original draft and current brief, not only a paraphrased reconstruction. Preserve facts, numbers, citations and their scope, deadlines, conditional commitments, exact quotations, required boilerplate and useful voice. Separate sources and samples. Identify material findings, optional preferences, what should remain, and unresolved facts. Do not transfer reviewer conjecture as fact or an editorial proposal as an approved new commitment.

Review suggestions are provisional until checked against the current brief. BGM Humanize It should decline a proposed change that damages fidelity, even if BGM AI Check recommended it. No mandatory critique-edit-critique loop applies to ordinary work. Check the revised passages once against the original and stop when the requested improvement is achieved. Respect copy-only, explanation, HTML and side-by-side requests. Neither review nor revision authorises sending, publishing or external detector uploads.
